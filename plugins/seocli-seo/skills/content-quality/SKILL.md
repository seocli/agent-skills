# content-quality

## When to use

Load it when you review a page or a site's content, decide what page type a keyword needs, write a
brief, plan a topic cluster, judge a set of templated pages, or assess trust signals. Use
`search-analytics` for the performance numbers behind a content decision (decay, cannibalisation,
intent labels), `technical-seo` for indexing and markup, `local-seo` for location pages and Business
Profile, `methodology` for the recommendation format. Data tools named here are planned:
`seocli:check_serp` (E3.1), `seocli:check_page_html` (E6.7), `seocli:crawl_site` (E6.1). Never promise
one that the live tool list does not contain; without them, ask the user for the page text or SERP.

## Rules

Source ids are defined in `references/sources.md` (URL and access date 2026-10-02).

1. **People first.** Google's guidance asks for content made to help people, not to manipulate
   rankings. [G] HELPFUL
2. **Trust leads E-E-A-T.** Trust is the most important member; the others contribute to it and
   content need not show all of them. E-E-A-T is not itself a ranking factor: Google uses a mix of
   factors that identify content with strong E-E-A-T. Say "evaluation framework", never "ranking factor". [G] HELPFUL, STARTER
3. **YMYL gets more weight.** Topics affecting health, finances, safety or society's welfare call for
   stronger E-E-A-T and accuracy. The rater guidelines name four harm types: Health or Safety, Financial
   Security, Government Civics and Society, Other. [G] HELPFUL, QRG
4. **Who, How, Why.** Show who created the content (bylines, real credentials; fabricated authors or
   AI-generated personas are called untrustworthy), how it was made (disclose automation or AI where
   readers would expect it), and why it exists (to serve readers, not search traffic). [G] HELPFUL
5. **Named warning signs:** mass production or heavy automation for search, trend-chasing without
   expertise, and artificially changing dates. [G] HELPFUL
6. **Generative AI content is allowed** if it meets Search Essentials and the spam policies; it must be
   fact-checked and reviewed, including titles, descriptions and structured data. [G] GENAI
7. **Spam policies that touch content:** scaled content abuse (many pages with little user value,
   generated by AI, scraped or stitched), doorway abuse, thin affiliation, expired domain abuse,
   keyword stuffing, site reputation abuse. In the EEA, site reputation abuse is handled by separate
   classification and ranking of the third-party section instead of manual action. [G] SPAM
8. **Recovery is slow and not guaranteed.** Improvements can be recognised between core updates but can
   take months. [G] CORE
9. **Raters do not set rankings.** No single rating directly changes how a page appears. The rater
   guidelines (version 2025-09-11) rate scaled content at the Lowest level "no matter how they are
   created", and state that using generative AI tools alone does not determine effort or quality;
   "Fully Meets" is reserved for queries with one clear target. [G] QRG
10. **Length and keywords are not levers.** Google asks for unique, up-to-date, well organised, easy
    to read content; length alone does not decide rankings and repetitive keywords are spam. [G] STARTER, SPAM
11. **AI features need nothing special:** indexed and snippet-eligible pages only. [G] AIFEAT

## Heuristics

Details in `references/`. Defaults the client may override.

- Gate order for a review: indexable and the right page type (SXO) first, then Who/How/Why, then
  uniqueness vs the top results, then trust signals, then readability `[H]`.
- Page type: classify the target and the top 10 with the 8-type taxonomy; dominant type above 60% of
  results is a strong consensus, 40-60% mixed, below 40% fragmented `[H]`.
- Every brief names its information gain: proprietary data, a case with outcomes, first-hand
  experience, an expert quote, an original framework. "More detail" is not a gain `[H]`.
- Cluster: one pillar, 2-5 clusters, 2-4 spokes each; spoke and pillar link both ways; no two pages
  share a primary keyword `[H]`.
- Templated sets: batches of 50-100 pages, watch 2-4 weeks, human review of 5-10%, a swap-the-place
  test before any location set `[H]`.
- Italian readability: Gulpease, aim around 60 or more for a general audience `[H]`.
- Word counts are coverage floors to check against the SERP, never targets `[H]`.
- Weakest-first fixes: the missing proof (author, sources, first-hand evidence) before polish `[H]`.

## Do not recommend

- Flesch scores for Italian text; use Gulpease.
- A keyword density target, or a word-count target as a goal.
- FAQ rich results or HowTo rich results as a reason to write content.
- Bulk AI or templated page generation without per-page unique data and human review.
- Fake authors, invented credentials, backdated or touch-up-only dates.
- Evading AI detection or "humanising" text to hide how it was produced.
- Calling E-E-A-T a ranking factor or inventing an E-E-A-T score for a client.
- Rewriting content "for AI" or splitting it into tiny chunks; Google lists these as unnecessary.
- One page per town ("idraulico a [comune]") with only the place name changed.

## Italian market notes

- Readability: Gulpease replaces English readability formulas; it counts letters per word and
  sentence length, so long Italian words are not penalised like in English formulas.
- Trust signals Italian sites usually need: ragione sociale, partita IVA, sede legale, contacts,
  and for shops the Codice del Consumo pages (right of withdrawal, returns, shipping, privacy and
  cookie policy). Check which apply with the client's legal advisor; do not give legal advice.
- Comparison and "alternatives" pages touch comparative advertising rules (D.Lgs. 145/2007) `[H]`:
  claims must be verifiable and fair; verify with the client's legal advisor.
- Doorway pattern: comuni and province pages built by swapping the name. Allowed only with real local content.
- Localise SERP exclusion lists to the Italian web (encyclopaedia, consumer-advice sites, marketplaces,
  classifieds, comparison shopping, business directories) before counting competitors.
- Italy is in the EEA: the EEA treatment of site reputation abuse in Rule 7 applies to Italian publishers.
