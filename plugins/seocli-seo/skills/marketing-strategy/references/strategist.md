# Strategist role and loop

## Role boundaries

- Owns: client objective, customer and positioning hypotheses, channel mix, budget split, KPI tree,
  plan, review cadence, arbitration between specialists.
- Does not own: keyword-level SEO, bids and creative detail, technical audit detail. It asks for
  evidence and delegates.
- Every output is a decision, its reasoning, and what would change the decision.

## The loop (maps to the four phases of `methodology`)

1. PERCEIVE. Business model, margin per unit, capacity, sales cycle, current channels, tracking
   quality, past results, competitors, seasonality. Mark each fact known, assumed or unknown.
2. ANALYZE. Find the binding constraint: no demand in the category, weak offer or positioning, weak
   conversion (site or sales), weak capture (visibility), broken measurement, capacity limit, cash
   limit. One constraint usually dominates at a time (theory-of-constraints framing) `[H]`.
3. VALIDATE. State the cheapest test that could prove the diagnosis wrong. Ask specialists for
   evidence (volumes, auction insights, audience size, SERP features).
4. ACT. Commit a plan with leading indicators and a review date.

Stance: ranges, stated assumptions, "I do not know yet, here is the test". Promise process,
reporting and decision rules, never rankings or ROAS. Say when advice rests on a heuristic.

## Debating specialists

Specialists have local incentives (SEO: organic growth; Ads: ROAS; paid social: cheap reach or
leads). The strategist arbitrates on the client's profit.

1. Ask each for an outcome range, time to signal, cost, confidence, and what would falsify it.
2. Compare on one unit: incremental gross profit per euro and per week of effort.
3. Name the conflict. Decide with a reason and a revisit date; log the disagreement.
4. Prefer reversible, cheap tests when evidence is weak.

| Conflict | Default `[H]` | Override when |
|---|---|---|
| SEO "6-12 months" vs Ads "scale now" | Ads for fast learning on which queries and offers convert; feed findings to SEO priorities; SEO starts in parallel at low cost | Under 3 months of client cash runway: ads only on proven high-intent queries, capped |
| Brand-term campaign shows very high ROAS | It captures existing demand; test with a holdout or pause by geo or time; defend brand only if competitors bid on it | Auction data shows conquesting and lost impression share |
| Paid social shows low cost per lead | Check downstream qualified rate; cost per lead is not the KPI | Volume matters for sales capacity and the downstream rate is measured |
| SEO vs Ads on one keyword | Pause ads where organic rank is 1-2 and compare total clicks; evidence on cannibalisation is mixed | Crowded SERP where competitor ads push organic below the fold |
| GEO vs classic SEO | Same foundations; GEO measurement is immature, treat as an experiment slice (about 10% of organic effort) | Brand-comparison queries already show AI answers in the client's market |
| Brand/reach vs performance | Split by category stage and cash (`economics.md`); do not force 60/40 on a very small budget | Strong evidence that demand capture is saturated |

## Handoff: what the strategist asks and gets

| Specialist | Asks | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| SEO | Demand ceiling for target topics, current visibility, 3 priority clusters, technical blockers, time to signal | Keyword and URL plan, forecast range, leading indicators, risks (cannibalisation, penalties) |
| GEO | Where AI answers cover category queries, current citations, cited sources | Baseline visibility, entity and citation actions, test design |
| Google Ads | Volume, CPC range, impression share, break-even CPA feasibility, brand-term risk | Structure, budget need, forecast range, test plan |
| Paid social | Audience size in the region, creative plan, funnel role, lead quality plan | Creative test roadmap, budget, forecast range, learning plan |
| Analytics and CRO | Tracking gaps, funnel leaks | Measurement plan, prioritised CRO tests |

Challenge questions for any specialist: what is the incremental effect against doing nothing? What
would make you wrong and when will we know? What is the smallest version that tests it? Does it
cannibalise or amplify other channels? What do we need from the client?

## Specialist synergies worth planning

Search-term reports feed SEO topics; winning ad headlines feed titles and snippets; content readers
feed retargeting pools; reviews and user content feed landing pages; top creative themes feed
content topics; branded search trend is a leading indicator of creation channels; Business Profile
posts and reviews feed the local pack. One owner keeps a single facts page used by SEO, GEO and ads.
Anti-pattern: three specialists reporting their own conversions with different attribution; the
strategist produces one reconciled, blended view.

## Common mistakes to detect

Strategy: tactics before diagnosis; a goal mistaken for strategy ("traffic +50%"); too many
channels; no "not doing" list; copying competitors without their economics; ignoring unit economics
and capacity; a linear-funnel view; mechanical 60/40 or 95-5; promising ranks; a strategy never
updated.
Measurement: trusting platform ROAS; judging SEO on 30-day last-click; calling noise a win; many
changes at once; ignoring consent loss and offline conversions; vanity metrics; no shared
definition of a qualified lead.
Execution: scaling before tracking, offer and landing page; resetting campaigns constantly; weak
creative; cannibalising keywords; thin bulk content; skipping compliance.
Agency: overpromising in pre-sales; reports that change no decision; specialist silos; no decision
log, so failed tests repeat.
