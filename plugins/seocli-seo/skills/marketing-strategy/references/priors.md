# Marketing-science priors and their caveats

All items here are `[H]` priors. They orient a conversation; none is a budget formula and none is
evidence about one Italian small client.

## Mental and physical availability (Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass)

- Claims: brands grow mainly by adding buyers (penetration) more than by raising loyalty; small
  brands have fewer buyers and slightly lower loyalty (double jeopardy); mental availability is the
  chance of being noticed in buying situations (category entry points, distinctive assets); physical
  availability is ease of finding and buying; reach light buyers, they make most sales.
- Caveats: strongest for repeat-purchase goods and some services, weaker for rare high-ticket,
  B2B and new categories. The "differentiation is a myth" claim is disputed. A new entrant with no
  awareness often benefits from a narrow niche first: niche to enter, broaden to grow.
- Use for small clients: list category entry points (the situations that trigger a purchase:
  "boiler broke", "wedding in six months") and cover many, not one slogan; build cheap distinctive
  assets (colour, name, a visual device used everywhere); physical availability online means
  findable on search, maps, marketplaces and WhatsApp, and easy to book or buy, often the cheapest win.

## Brand and activation balance (Binet and Field, 60/40)

- Finding: in a dataset of 996 UK award-entered campaigns 1980-2010, the best average split was about
  60% brand building to 40% activation by budget; brand effects are slow and compound, activation is
  sharp and decays; short-termism erodes long-run results.
- Caveats: self-selected winners, survivorship, large advertisers, mass-reach media. The ratio varies
  by category (lower brand share for B2B and short consideration). "Brand" there is not a small firm's
  social posts. Never quote 60/40 as a law to a small client.
- Use: if a client spends 100% on bottom-funnel search and growth has plateaued, test moving a slice
  into reach or demand creation, with a hypothesis card.

## The 95-5 idea

- Claim: at any time most B2B buyers are out of market (about 95%) and few in market (about 5%), so
  prime the many while capturing the few.
- Caveat: the 5% is an extrapolation from typical purchase cycles, not a measured constant, and was
  popularised by a platform that sells B2B ads. Consumer cycles differ (a pizza weekly, a mortgage
  every few years).
- Use the client's own number: in-market share is roughly months actively searching divided by months
  between purchases. Boiler replacement (about one month every 12 years) gives under 1%: capture can
  never deliver much, creation and presence matter. Pizza delivery gives a large share.

## Demand capture and creation

Capture: brand and non-brand search ads, SEO on transactional queries, business profile,
marketplaces, retargeting; ceiling is existing demand. Creation: reach on social and video, PR,
partnerships, problem-awareness content, local presence. Size the demand ceiling first: addressable
searches per month times click and conversion rates times margin. If that is below spend, the
channel is too big.

## Messy middle

Between trigger and purchase people loop between exploring and evaluating, not a straight funnel.
Biases tested: social proof, authority, category heuristics, free, scarcity, now. Implication: be
present in both modes (guides, comparisons, reviews, clear price and availability) and make
evaluation easy. Caveat: a search-platform commissioned study on consumer e-commerce; use as a
checklist, not proof.

## Positioning (Dunford) in one session

1. List real alternatives the customer uses today, including "do nothing", "a cousin who does it",
   a spreadsheet, the competitor around the corner.
2. List the client's attributes those alternatives lack (specific, verifiable).
3. For each, the customer value and the proof (reviews, numbers, certificates, cases).
4. Who cares most: the target segment (who, trigger, job to be done).
5. Pick the category frame that makes the value obvious.
6. Test: could a competitor copy the claim in five minutes? Then it is not positioning. Adjectives
   ("quality, passion, experience") are not positioning.

## Channel selection (Bullseye)

Brainstorm all channels, rank into three tiers, test 3-6 cheaply, double down on the one that works.
In pre-sales propose 2-3 channels with a small test budget and pass or fail thresholds each. Local
businesses have a short realistic menu: business profile, search ads, local SEO, paid social lead
ads and retargeting, email and WhatsApp, reviews, local partnerships.
